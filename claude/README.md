# claude

While I've developed most of the code here by hand, I started using Claude Code in April 2026 and have been cautiously using it to add new features. "Cautiously" because this codebase is complex, and I've yet to develop perfect instincts for how much heavy lifting you can entrust a chatbot with.

For now, this folder contains instructions for various subprojects that Claude has assisted me with. It's in this repository mainly to show potential employers how I'm integrating AI tools into my working process. Because, as it seems from the current brutal job market, software developers who *AREN'T* doing that might be something analogous to horse cavalry charging a heavy machine gun emplacement in World War One. Well-trained and skilled, perhaps, but for the wrong century.

## Thoughts about Claude

_My answer, as posted on social media, to the question "Is Claude Code replacing developers, or just changing what developers do?"_

For now, I’d say the answer is “both”.

I’ve been using Claude Code extensively for my own personal projects. I compare the experience to having my own employee, a young-ish software developer with a computer science degree from a reputable university and ten years of industry experience. It’s that smart. Except, it gets stuff done a lot faster than a human being. There’s no work that it finds unpleasant, boring, or meaningless. You don’t feel the need to impress it or look out for its happiness, as you do with people. You can make mistakes and not worry about it judging you (well, for now — corporations may yet use it to monitor or “rate” employees).

A recent project of mine had to do with creating a framework that could pull financial data from different brokerage sites. I’d already made a wrapper for the API of Brokerage A and wanted a wrapper that would do the same thing for Brokerage B, which had its own proprietary API. I told Claude what I wanted in an instructions file, and had it complete the work in stages. It looked at my wrapper from Broker A and created nearly identical functionality for Broker B. The only differences had to do with differences in the information that the two brokerages were willing to provide in the first place.

Claude did a nearly flawless job. It’s great at the kind of “code something to these specifications” tasks that you would have previously given to someone not that many years out of college, work that would have taken them somewhere between half a day and two weeks. I’ve completed several two-week projects in less than a day apiece.

On the “replacing developers” side of the equation, there’s no question that Claude is a lot better than a recently graduated 22-year-old junior developer in terms of its skillset and tirelessness. In the pre-chatbot days, companies would hire juniors more as investments than assets. With proper mentoring and guidance, a junior would grow into their role over a year or so (or not!). Within about five years, they’d be a pretty seasoned coder. Now, though, there’s a strong incentive **NOT** to invest in “growing” young developers. Claude is simply better in terms of coding-to-spec work, faster and cheaper. It’s troubling to me because these tools are probably going to improve **A LOT** over the next five years, further adding to this reality. Without the financial incentive of being able to find a high-paying job straight out of school, fewer young people will bother to learn programming or computer science in much depth. Because I’m a Gen-Xer who went through all that, along with a shit-ton of experience in industry, I have the skillset to be able to check Claude’s work, to steer it in the right direction. I can look at the code it generates and understand it quickly.

But who’s going to take the place of people like me in the years to come? Will AI just do **EVERYTHING**?

On the “changing what developers do” side, for a skilled senior-level software engineer like me, using Claude is like getting your hands on a videogame cheat code that gives you all the powerups, letting you speed through the game world and dispose of enemies with ease. It does a lot of tedious grunt work very quickly, allowing me to be a lot more ambitious in my goals. More of my attention goes to “what do I want this software to do?” and less of it to “how the hell do I do this?” Claude takes care of that part. If I’d had this thing five years ago, and no one else did, I would have been a superstar, commanding a huge paycheck. Now, though, every experienced developer has the same superpowers. Hard to say how this will change things in the long run.

As with programming itself, a “garbage in, garbage out” rule applies to Claude. If you give it overly vague or poorly-written instructions, you’re not necessarily going to get what you want. If you hand it a bunch of badly-commented spaghetti code and tell it to add a new feature, it might get confused (as also often happens with people). It will happily create a big, sprawling monstrosity if you don’t impose your own structure and architecture on the codebase. Not to mention some discipline upon junior developers who just want to vibe code everything and don’t always perceive that Claude’s default solution to commonly-occurring problems might not be the best fit for the company’s needs.

To sum up, I’d say Claude’s a massive game-changer for people who are already highly experienced. For those who aren’t, it makes the job market a lot tougher than five years ago. A bigger issue is that this tech isn’t standing still. Chatbots have come a long way in just a few years, and there are many potential pathways for improving them. The incentives for success are huge, both for the companies building models and for governments looking to stay ahead of their national rivals in a new AI arms race. Remember every other arms race in recent history? The battleship, the airplane, the atomic bomb, the first to get weapons into orbit… Except this arms race is to build something that’s smarter than us. Maybe not in every sense of the word “smart”, but in enough of them. How's that going to turn out? I'm not sure that it's within anyone's ability to know.
